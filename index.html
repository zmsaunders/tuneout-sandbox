How this is calculated

The income tax side

The Ohio SD 100, traditional base: start from IT 1040 line 5 (Ohio AGI minus exemptions, never below zero), add back the business income deduction from the Schedule of Adjustments line 13, multiply by 0.5%.

Exemptions use the 2025 amounts: $2,400 per person up to $40,000 of modified AGI, $2,150 to $80,000, $1,900 above that, and $0 once modified AGI reaches $500,000 (the threshold set in R.C. 5747.025 for 2026 and later). The state indexes these each year, so 2027's figures will be a little higher.

Ohio's retirement income credit, senior citizen credit, joint filing credit, and the zero bracket below $26,050 all live on the IT 1040. None of them touch the SD 100. A household that owes Ohio nothing can still owe this.

Pensions, IRA and 401(k) withdrawals, interest, dividends, capital gains, and rental income are all in Ohio AGI and all taxed. Social Security is not. If you work in the district but live outside it, you pay nothing.

The property tax side

Two state laws signed in December 2025 change Miamisburg school taxes regardless of this vote, so they appear on both receipts.

HB 186 gives a credit to homeowners in districts sitting at the 20-mill floor. The county auditor estimates $80 per $100,000 of value for Miamisburg, first appearing on second-half 2026 bills and growing after that.

HB 129 counts emergency and substitute levies toward the 20-mill floor starting in tax year 2026, the year Montgomery County reappraises. Miamisburg carries about 17 mills of those levies, so it comes off the floor and its voted levies go back under HB 920 reduction factors. The district's treasurer puts the revenue loss at $13.5 million over four years. What that means for one bill isn't certified until December; the dropdown lets you try sizes. One mill is $35 per $100,000 of appraised value.

HB 96 eliminated new emergency, substitute, and replacement levies as of Jan. 1, 2026. Ordinary voted operating levies remain legal, and Miamisburg's existing 17 mills can be renewed once more as fixed-sum levies for at most five years. The replacement-levy row on the "fails" receipt is arithmetic: the district's stated need (about $7 million a year) divided by an estimated tax base. Change either in CONFIG.

What still needs verifying
  • District taxable valuation for the replacement-levy mills. The 2025 abstract from the county auditor will settle it.
  • The district's actual reduction plan if the levy fails, and any fee schedule. Nothing is assumed here; the fields default to zero.
  • The tax year 2026 rate sheet, once the budget commission certifies it, to replace the HB 129 dropdown with a number.
  • The certified ballot language from the Board of Elections.

Every assumption lives in CONFIG at the top of the script.
